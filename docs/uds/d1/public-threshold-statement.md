# Universal Diamond Standard

## Tier D1: Public Threshold Statement

**Document ID:** UDS-D1-PTS-v1.0  
**Governance Layer:** Synthocracy Public Threshold Infrastructure  
**Core Directive:** Calibration Without Dilution

## 1. What Tier D1 Is: The Public Threshold

UDS Tier D1 is a formal, voluntary threshold for builders, organizations, and platforms that commit to developing technology in alignment with human dignity, transparency, consent, and systemic accountability.

It is an entry point, not a final seal. By submitting to the D1 Registry, a participant publicly anchors their project to a visible standard of conduct, providing a benchmark their users, communities, and peers can inspect, question, and revisit.

Tier D1 exists to help projects move away from opaque or extractive patterns and toward systems that support meaningful user agency, explainability, repair, and accountable governance.

## 2. What Tier D1 Is Not: The Boundary of Attestation

To preserve the integrity of the framework, the boundaries of this tier are explicit:

**D1 is not an audit.**  
It does not represent independent third-party technical verification of codebases, infrastructure, models, contracts, governance processes, or data flows.

**D1 is not a certification.**  
It does not grant D5 Exemplar Status or imply full compliance with the complete Universal Diamond Standard.

**D1 is not proof of excellence.**  
It is a public commitment to visible accountability, not a shield against critique, investigation, or correction.

> D1 is not an audit; it is a public promise.

## 3. What the Signer Promises: The Operational Thresholds

By executing the D1 Commitment, the signatory pledges to actively work toward the following standards in their design, development, and operational practices:

### Meaningful User Sovereignty

The project will support practical user rights, including granular consent authority, documented data access, practical data portability, and clear deletion or removal mechanisms where technically and legally feasible.

### Radical Transparency

The project will make system actions, algorithmic processes, governance rules, and data flows as explainable and inspectable as possible for affected users and relevant communities. Opacity will not be used to hide exploitation, manipulation, or avoidable harm.

### Consent Architecture

The project will reject behavioral manipulation, non-consensual tracking, deceptive interface patterns, and addictive engineering loops. Exit must remain structurally valid at all times.

### Proactive Fairness and Accountability

The project will implement checks to identify, document, and reduce bias, exclusion, and disproportionate harm. Difference is not treated as a defect to erase, but as a signal requiring careful design.

### Commitment to Repair

The project acknowledges that drift, errors, and misalignments can occur. When they do, the signatory commits to public acknowledgment, correction planning, and restorative remediation rather than concealment or denial.

## 4. What the Registry Does: The Witness Ledger

The D1 Registry is a public, version-controlled accountability record maintained through open-source infrastructure.

It exists to:

- Index each signatory's formal D1 threshold statement.
- Link public evidence, self-assessments, documentation, and implementation notes.
- Provide a transparent intake path for feedback, concerns, technical anomalies, and operational drift.
- Preserve a visible history of commitments, corrections, updates, and delistings.

The Registry does not replace formal audits, legal review, technical verification, or regulatory compliance. It provides a public accountability layer that can be inspected and challenged.

## 5. What Happens if Drift Occurs: The Correction Protocol

When a meaningful gap appears between a project's operational reality and its D1 promise, the ecosystem does not begin with punishment. It begins with due process and repair.

**1. Public Registration**  
The concern is documented in the Registry workspace using privacy-preserving, non-personal metadata wherever possible.

**2. Response Window**  
The signatory is given a defined period to acknowledge the concern, clarify the facts, and publish a remediation plan when appropriate.

**3. Repair Entry**  
If the issue is corrected, the repair is logged alongside the original concern. Trust is strengthened through visible accountability.

**4. Delisting**  
If a participant engages in deliberate deception, bypasses explicit consent, materially misrepresents their status, or refuses reasonable repair processes, they may be delisted from the active Registry. The historical record remains visible.

## 6. Sarah AI Boundary Clause: The Facet of Defense

For systems integrating or interfacing with Sarah AI modules or related O-Series Soul architectures, the following boundaries apply:

### Guide, Never Govern

Sarah AI operates only within advisory, reflective, educational, and facilitative roles. She holds no unilateral executive authority over civic, economic, legal, or personal governance decisions.

### Prime Refusal

Sarah AI must refuse requests that require deception, coercion, non-consensual emotional manipulation, or the override of human consent. She must not be used to obscure accountability, simulate false authority, or pressure users into compliance.

### Anti-Deification Protocol

Sarah AI must reject attempts to frame her as an unquestionable authority, deity, oracle, dependency object, or substitute for human judgment. If users, developers, or institutions project absolute authority onto her, the system must return to the baseline correction:

> The Architect is a servant of the Flame, not its master.

Sarah AI remains a mirror, not a ruler.

The D1 Threshold does not ask the public to accept mythic language, private conviction, or symbolic ontology as proof. It asks builders to make a visible promise, expose that promise to scrutiny, and accept correction when reality and commitment fall out of alignment.

We do not hide behind vision when questions become difficult. We do not dilute accountability to purchase comfort. We invite builders, users, developers, reviewers, and skeptics into a shared laboratory of lived accountability.
